# Location research record (2026-11)

Sources actually read: Wikipedia pages for each city, plus city sites where they loaded. Wikipedia text was partly truncated, so some facts came from a model summary of the remainder; those were used only for low-risk references. Boca Raton's city site returned HTTP 403 and Coconut Creek's site refused the connection, so neither city site was read.

## Lighthouse Point (Broward)
Verified: most of the city built on canals dug in the 1950s and 1960s (waterfront housing); Intracoastal Waterway; Hillsboro Inlet and the lighthouse (in Hillsboro Beach) the city is named after; Venetian Isles and Beacon Light shopping centers; population 10,486 (2020); 5,856 housing units (2020); elevation 7 ft; borders Pompano Beach (W, S), Deerfield Beach (N), Hillsboro Beach (E across the ICW).
Not used (unverified): any named residential neighbourhoods or streets (sources named none).

## Deerfield Beach (Broward)
Verified: pier and beach on Deerfield Beach Island; many oldest structures from the 1920s in Spanish Mediterranean Revival style; rapid growth 1950-1980; Hillsboro River and Boca Raton to the north, Atlantic to the east, Coconut Creek to the west, Pompano Beach to the south; population 86,859 (2020).
From summary text (lower confidence): Hillsboro Boulevard, Federal Highway, Powerline Road, Sample Road, Dixie Highway, Military Trail; Crystal Lake, West Deerfield Beach, Pioneer Park; Deerfield Island Park, Quiet Waters Park; company headquarters in the city.

## Fort Lauderdale (Broward)
Verified: county seat and largest city in Broward (182,760 in 2020); about 165 miles of inland waterways ("Venice of America"); Rio Vista and Colee Hammock named; Stranahan House and Bonnet House (1895); city homepage states the city is vulnerable to flooding during king tides; more than 60 recognised neighbourhoods; bordering communities listed.
From summary text: Las Olas Boulevard, Sunrise Boulevard, Riverwalk, A1A; downtown is the largest central business district in Broward.

## Coconut Creek (Broward)
Verified: planned community; "Butterfly Capital of the World"; housing mostly single-family homes, condos and townhouses in landscaped communities; 26,971 housing units and 57,833 people (2020); about 1,400 businesses; Butterfly World at Tradewinds Park; The Promenade; Seminole Casino Coconut Creek; seceded from Pompano Beach in the 1960s; borders listed.
From web search (lower confidence): Lyons Road, Sample Road, Winston Park, Wynmoor (55+ gated).

## Coral Springs (Broward)
Verified: master-planned by Coral Ridge Properties; strict building codes and aesthetic rules (exterior paint colours etc.); rapid growth in the 1970s-1990s, residential build-out 2003; University Drive, Sample Road, Sawgrass Expressway; Coral Springs Covered Bridge (Florida's only covered bridge), Coral Square, Mullins Park, Corporate Park, downtown at Sample Road and University Drive; borders Parkland (N), Coconut Creek (E), Margate and North Lauderdale (SE), Tamarac (S); population 134,394 (2020).

## Boca Raton (Palm Beach County)
Verified: Palm Beach County; population 97,422 (2020); luxury beach culture; strict development code limiting commercial buildings and signs; Old Floresta Historic District (designated 1990); hurricanes 2004, 2005, 2017; West Boca Raton addresses outside city limits.
From summary text: Mizner Park, Town Center, Spanish River Park, FAU, Palmetto Park Road, Glades Road, Federal Highway, A1A, Military Trail, Congress Avenue; barrier island and Intracoastal.
Flag for owner: Boca Raton is at the edge of a 30-minute drive from Pompano Beach and is in a different county; confirm coverage.
